How this baseline is built

Each robot's 2026 quantity-1 BOM from its robot file is split into parts that add up to its stated total. Parts the file prices are used as stated; the rest are estimates and say so in the Note column.

Every part is mapped to one of the five modules in the Optimus cost projection (actuators, sensors, compute, structure, battery and power) and scaled by that module's cost ratio. Years between anchors use a constant yearly rate of decline, and costs hold at the 2040 floor through 2050.

Grippers, rail motors and motor drivers count as actuators, as they do in the Optimus model. LaundryBot's itemized parts fall short of its stated BOM, so the gap is carried as an Unitemized line under structure.

The curves assume humanoid-scale production. CleanSweep and TrashBot are already built from mass-market consumer parts (robot-vacuum and self-sealing-can components) and will fall less than shown. The cost map's own notes also flag the 2035 total and floor as below every published forecast, so treat 2035 onward as optimistic.
